Learning Mastery Protocol12-week, evidence-based training in learning how to learn

Train how you learn, then measure it

This site is your training system. The aim isn't to study harder. It's to swap low-yield habits (rereading, highlighting, cramming) for methods that research supports, and to check with data whether they're working for you.

An honest promise. Nobody can guarantee "10x learning" or a top-1% ranking. What you can expect from consistent retrieval practice and spacing is reliably better long-term retention than rereading, often by a large margin on delayed tests. Your dashboard will show whether that's happening for you.

Your path

1 · Intake & diagnostic

Tell the system about your habits and time, then take a 12-question test that also measures your calibration, meaning how well your confidence predicts your accuracy.

2 · First exercise

Read a short passage once, predict your score, then free-recall it. This sets your baseline for retention at days 1, 3, 7 and 30.

3 · Week 1 plan

Seven days of 30–60 minute sessions. For each activity you get what to do, how, why it works, and how to measure it.

Class practice

Multiple-choice practice built from your own lecture slides, notes and study guides. The picker spreads questions across every topic, brings back the ones you miss on a spaced schedule, never repeats a question in the same session, and shuffles the answer order each time so you can't memorize positions.

Diagnostic assessment

12 questions about how learning works. For each one, pick an answer and rate your confidence. The point isn't only your score. You'll also see whether your confidence was accurate. Answer first; explanations come after.

Exercise 1: Baseline free recall

This measures how much you keep from a single careful reading, which is what most people rely on. You'll retest the same material at days 1, 3, 7 and 30 to build your forgetting curve.

Review queue

Spaced repetition with adaptive intervals. Write each card as a question you must answer from memory, not a fact to reread. When you get a card right, its interval grows (1 → 3 → 7 → 16 → 35 days…). When you miss it, the interval resets to 1.

Calibration: predicted vs actual

Before each test, write down your predicted score. A gap near 0 means good calibration. A large positive gap means overconfidence, which is the usual "illusion of competence."

Four phases. You move on when you meet the gate criteria, not just because the calendar says so. Missing a gate means repeating the weakest week, which is normal and expected.

Core principles and the evidence behind them

Each method is graded by how well the research supports it. Strong means replicated across many labs and real classrooms. Promising means it works under some conditions or the evidence is thinner. Myth means it's popular but unsupported or refuted.
